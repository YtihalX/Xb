# $\chi_{c1}(4010)\to D\bar D^*$ width with the Cornell $2P$ wave function

**Claim.** With the Cornell $2P$ wave function in the LO bare–continuum vertex, the width of a bare $\chi_{c1}(2P)$ state at the $\chi_{c1}(4010)$ mass is $\Gamma[\chi_{c1}(4010)\to D\bar D^*]\approx14$ MeV ($11$–$17$ MeV across the mass uncertainty), about $4.5$ times below the measured $62.7$ MeV.

**Context.** The charm repeat of [@Zhang:2026iyr] in followup.tex reads $\chi_{c1}(4010)$ as the $\chi_{c1}(2P)$-dominated state. A colleague tested that reading with an order-of-magnitude estimate, $\Gamma\simeq20$ MeV, using $\psi_n(p)\approx\lambda^{-3/2}$ with $\lambda=100$ MeV and $p\approx272$ MeV. This brief redoes that estimate with the wave function the model actually uses.

## Summary

### The estimate

The vertex is that of [@Zhang:2026iyr] v1, Eqs. (52)–(53) (CONVENTIONS.md "Bare–continuum vertex"):

$$\langle D\bar D^*|\hat g|\chi_{c1}(4010)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}\,g\,\psi_2(p),\qquad \psi_n(p)=4\pi\int_0^\infty r^2dr\,j_0(pr)\,R_{1n}(r).$$

Converting to relativistic normalisation gives

$$g_{\rm rel}=\sqrt{2M\,2m_D\,2m_{D^*}}\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}\,g\,\psi_2(p),\qquad \Gamma=\frac{g_{\rm rel}^2\,p}{8\pi M^2},$$

with $M=4012.5$ MeV [@LHCb:2024vfz] v3.

The inputs are:

- $R_{12}$ is the $2P$ Cornell radial function for $m_c=1.328$ GeV (followup.tex).
- $\sigma=0.198$ GeV$^2$, $\gamma=-0.434$ and $g_l=35$ MeV are the static lattice values of [@Zhang:2026iyr] v1, Table I, taken from [@Bulava:2024jpj]. They are flavour independent at LO.
- The bare–continuum coupling is $g=2\sqrt{2/3}\,g_l=57.15$ MeV.
- The $D$ and $D^*$ masses are isospin averages of [@ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk].

### Decay momentum

$$\begin{aligned}\lambda(M^2,m_D^2,m_{D^*}^2)&=\big[M^2-(m_D+m_{D^*})^2\big]\big[M^2-(m_{D^*}-m_D)^2\big]\\&=(1.0783\times10^{6})(1.6080\times10^{7})\ \text{MeV}^4=1.7339\times10^{13}\ \text{MeV}^4,\end{aligned}$$

$$p=\frac{\lambda^{1/2}}{2M}=\frac{4.1640\times10^{6}\ \text{MeV}^2}{8025\ \text{MeV}}=518.9\ \text{MeV}.$$

| Channel | $M-m_D-m_{D^*}$ (MeV) | $p=\lambda^{1/2}/2M$ (MeV) | $\sqrt{2\mu(M-m_D-m_{D^*})}$ (MeV) | $p^2$ (GeV$^2$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| isospin average | 136.7 | 518.9 | 514.3 | 0.269 |
| $D^0\bar D^{*0}$ | 140.8 | 526.5 | 521.7 | 0.277 |
| $D^+D^{*-}$ | 132.6 | 511.1 | 506.8 | 0.261 |

In every channel the momentum is 511–527 MeV, never 272 MeV. Read literally with unsquared masses, the formula gives $\lambda(M,m_D,m_{D^*})=-1.5\times10^7$ MeV$^2$, which is negative and has no real root. The 272 MeV probably comes from the value of $p^2$ in GeV$^2$ written as $p$ in MeV, i.e. a square root that was never taken; this is a guess and not confirmed.

### Width

| Step | $p$ (MeV) | $\psi_2(p)$ (GeV$^{-3/2}$) | $g_{\rm rel}$ (GeV) | $\Gamma$ (MeV) |
|------------------|------|----------|------|----------|
| Colleague, as written | 272 | $\lambda^{-3/2}=31.6$ | 5 | 16.8 |
| Colleague, exact prefactor | 272 | 31.6 | 5.59 | 21.0 |
| Cornell $2P$, $p=272$ MeV | 272 | 61.9 | 10.95 | 80.6 |
| **Cornell $2P$, physical $p$** | **519** | **$-18.6$** | **3.29** | **13.9** |
| Measured [@LHCb:2024vfz] v3 | | | | $62.7^{+7.0+6.4}_{-6.4-6.6}$ |

The colleague rounds $g_{\rm rel}$ to $5$ GeV and quotes the resulting $16.8$ MeV as $\simeq20$ MeV. Varying $M$ by $\pm5.7$ MeV (the larger statistical and systematic errors combined in quadrature) moves the physical-$p$ result between $10.7$ and $17.2$ MeV.

### What changes

- **The decay momentum** is $519$ MeV rather than $272$ MeV, see above.
- **$\psi_2(p)$ has a node at $p=439$ MeV.** The physical momentum lies just above it, where $|\psi_2|=18.6$ GeV$^{-3/2}$. That is smaller than the estimate $31.6$ GeV$^{-3/2}$, and under a third of the $61.9$ GeV$^{-3/2}$ at $272$ MeV. The colleague's $\simeq20$ MeV is close to $14$ MeV only because the momentum slip and the wave-function estimate partly cancel.
- **The prefactor approximations are harmless.** $\sqrt{2M\,2m_D\,2m_{D^*}}\approx(2~\text{GeV})^{3/2}$ is a factor $3.9$ too small, and dropping $1/\sqrt{4\pi}$ makes it a factor $3.5$ too large. The net effect is $10^2g$ in place of the exact $97.9\,g$.
- **The result is fragile.** Because the physical momentum sits next to the node, anything that moves the node shifts the width: $m_c$, $\sigma$, $\gamma$, $1/m_c$ corrections, or the mass itself. Reaching $62.7$ MeV with this wave function would need $g\approx121$ MeV instead of $57$ MeV.

## Evidence

- **Checks:** these are cross-checks printed by the toy, not permanent checks.
  - An independent finite-difference radial solve ($L=1$, reduced mass $m_c/2$) reproduces the package's Cornell levels, $E_1=0.891918$ GeV and $E_2=1.394412$ GeV, and its $\psi_n(p)$ at both momenta to every printed digit.
  - The nonrelativistic golden rule with measure $d^3p/(2\pi)^3$, $\Gamma=\mu p\,g^2\psi_2^2/(4\pi^2)$, gives $14.4$ MeV. That agrees with the relativistic $13.9$ MeV up to the expected factor $(m_D+m_{D^*})/M=0.966$.
  - The width formula with the colleague's rounded $g_{\rm rel}=5$ GeV and $p=272$ MeV gives $16.8$ MeV, which they quote as $\simeq20$ MeV.
  - With $V_0=-36$ MeV (followup.tex), the bare $1P$ and $2P$ masses are $3511.9$ and $4014.4$ MeV, within 2 MeV of $M[\chi_{c1}(1P)]$ and $M[\chi_{c1}(4010)]$.
- **Red runs:** none. This is a toy model, so the evidence is weaker than a red-run-backed check.
- **Sources:** [@Zhang:2026iyr] v1, Eqs. (52)–(53) and Table I, with the $Y_{00}=1/\sqrt{4\pi}$ normalisation at Xb.tex:890–891; [@LHCb:2024vfz] v3; [@ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk].

## Door

**Door:** two-way.

This is an LO golden-rule estimate that nothing builds on yet. The definitive width comes from the above-threshold pole, $\Gamma=-2\operatorname{Im}E_{\rm pole}$, which followup.tex lists as preliminary work.

**Blast radius:** the deferred charm-sector grilling (PLAN.md, line 91; decisions/0003), which takes $\chi_{c1}(4010)$ as the $\chi_{c1}(2P)$-dominated state to fix $m_c$. No slice or paper section depends on it.

## Approximations

- The bare $2P$ state is used at the physical mass, so there is no dressing, no compact probability below 1, and no energy dependence of $\psi_2$ across the width.
- Only the $S$-wave $D\bar D^*$ channel is included, with no OME, contact term, or mixing with the $1P$, $3P$ or $X(3872)$. The $D^*$ is treated as stable.
- $\sigma$, $\gamma$ and $g$ take their bottom-sector (static) values.
- The Cornell wave function is LO.
