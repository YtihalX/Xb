- **Path quadrature (S40).** Plain Gauss–Legendre on the three segments converges slowly, because a propagator pole sits within the margin of a segment about 4 GeV long. Over the Stage-3 scan at 64 nodes per segment the worst relative error of $J_\alpha$ is $1.6\times10^{-1}$, $7.6\times10^{-2}$, $1.8\times10^{-2}$, $1.1\times10^{-3}$ for margins 5, 10, 20, 40 MeV; for $t$ it is $7.5\times10^{-3}$ to $5.3\times10^{-5}$. At $-22.62-10i$ MeV with a 20 MeV margin, 128 nodes per segment give $2.7\times10^{-7}$. A throwaway test that splits the same polygon geometrically towards every pole (steps of twice the margin, to the segment ends) reaches $7.7\times10^{-12}$ with 392 nodes. Decide before Stage 6 how the path quadrature is graded.
  Deep paths (2026-11-13; plot, not a check: `notes/07-continued-lse/deep_paths.py`, figures `deep_paths_geometry.png`, `deep_paths_limits.png`). A larger margin in the same S40 builder deepens the path. At $-22.62-10i$ MeV it admits up to $d_k=197.6$ MeV, a path of depth $271.8$ MeV with a first leg of slope $0.996$. The cap is the slope-1 line through the origin, which separates flipped from unflipped poles: $+k_5$ and $-k_1$ both lie about 198 MeV from it. The worst loop error at 64 nodes per segment drops from $3.4\times10^{-4}$ ($d_k=20$) to $1.0\times10^{-8}$ ($d_k=100$) and $4.4\times10^{-13}$ ($d_k=197$); the replica reproduces the package's vertices and errors. At Im $E=-10$ MeV the builder admits $d_k\ge m_\pi/\sqrt2$ on 81% of $\operatorname{Re}E\in[-88,45]$ MeV.
  The margin vanishes only just above each threshold. Paths with slopes at most 1 stay above the slope-1 line ($|h(x)|\le x$), and the newly flipped pole tends to that line as $\operatorname{Re}E\to\delta_\alpha^+$. The largest margin is then that pole's distance to the line, $(\operatorname{Re}k_\alpha-|\operatorname{Im}k_\alpha|)/\sqrt2$: at Im $E=-10$ MeV, $0.6$, $11.5$ and $34.2$ MeV at $0.05$, $1$ and $3$ MeV above the threshold. Just below, the unflipped pole lies under the line and the margin stays about 104 MeV. At Im $E=-1$ MeV it stays about 47 MeV, limited there by $|k_\alpha|\approx73$ MeV near $q=0$.
  Pole subtraction (Sokhotski–Plemelj) on the deformed path: route 3 (user's proposal, 2026-11-13; plot evidence, `notes/07-continued-lse/onshell_lhc.py`, `onshell_lhc_routes.png`). The path runs through every flipped pole, $0\to k_{(1)}\to\dots\to\Lambda$ sorted by $\operatorname{Re}k$; at each vertex the pole is subtracted and the subtracted term integrated along $\mathcal C$ below $k_F$ in closed form ($-i\pi/2k$ on $[0,\infty)$). With $k_F$ on $\mathcal C$, $V(k_F,q)$ is the kernel at two path points, so S34 and S35 keep its singular points off $\mathcal C$ along any homotopy of graph paths with slopes at most 1. Such a path exists: the flipped poles lie between $[0,\infty)$ and the slope-1 line and, at fixed $E$, on $\operatorname{Re}k\,|\operatorname{Im}k|=\mu_\alpha\Gamma/2$; the largest slope on the threshold grid below is $0.998$. On App. D's $J(p;m)$ with $p=k_5$, $m=m_\pi$, an on-shell kernel times the propagator, route 3 is exact to 30 digits for $\Gamma=2$–$40$ MeV, 1 MeV above the PV threshold. With fixed Gauss–Legendre nodes at $\Gamma=20$ MeV it gives $8\times10^{-15}$ at 24 and $5\times10^{-16}$ at 32 nodes per segment, both 0.05 and 1 MeV above the threshold; route 1 (the S40 path at $d_k=d_{k,\max}/2$, plain nodes) gives $6.2\times10^{-3}$ and $6.6\times10^{-11}$ at 256. In the LSE it needs one on-shell node per flipped channel (Haftel–Tabakin augmentation). Not yet in note 07.
  What the on-shell kernel requires (corrected 2026-11-13; the earlier reading is in `history/LOG.md`). A route is right when no singular point of $V(k_F,q)$ crosses its contour during the continuation (S32, first condition). Up points ($k_F+im_\pi$ and the three-body points beside it) lie above $[0,\Lambda]$ on the physical sheet and cross it once $|\operatorname{Im}k_F|$ is large enough. That crosses the real path, route 2 ($[0,\Lambda]$ plus the pole term plus App. D's cut terms, the way of the on-shell-point identities: S32's second condition), not a deformed path. Down points ($k_F-im_\pi$ and its neighbours) start below the path and must stay below it: S32's depth window.

  Proposal (2026-11-13; draft `notes/07-continued-lse/s40-proposal.tex`, figure `sp_rule.png` from `sp_rule.py`; not adopted). One rule for every energy region.   The on-shell node in the proposal (2026-11-13; draft, L3 and L5; `onshell_topt.py`, figure `onshell_topt.png`; numerical evidence, not a recorded check). At the node itself $Z^{(\beta)}_-(k_F,k_F)=E_{3,\beta}-\delta_F+c_{F\beta}(E-\delta_F)$ with $|c_{F\beta}|\le0.0043$, at least $92.8$ MeV for the pion orderings that occur.   Sheet as a sign (user's proposal, 2026-11-13; `notes/07-continued-lse/sp_sheets.py`, figure `sp_sheets.png`; numerical evidence, not a recorded check). With SP the sheet is one sign per channel, $\pm i\pi$ in the closed form, and the propagator poles do not constrain the contour. The pole condition $D$ needs an on-shell node only for a pole the contour passes on the side the sheet does not ask for, or runs through. The windows of those nodes and the moving singular points are the only constraints on the contour.

  Consistency of the sheet-as-a-sign form (2026-11-13; draft `s40-proposal.tex`, Sec. "Sheet as a sign", L7; a proof sketch, not checked by an independent route). For $\operatorname{Im}E<0$, $\operatorname{Re}E<47.58$ MeV and a sheet $\sigma$, the valid paths are the graph paths in the lower half-plane with slopes at most 1. For every flipped channel the up points of $V(k_\alpha,q)$ must lie above the path; for every unflipped channel the down points below it. These conditions bind only for poles passed on the side opposite to the sheet's, where they are the node windows; this is the criterion of the rectangle check above. With all windows imposed for every node instead, the VV-seam rectangle on $(-,-,+)$ fails at 31 of 81 energies; the unused node's window is not needed. Each condition is linear in the height function, so the valid set is convex and straight-line interpolation joins any two valid paths. Along it SP absorbs every pole crossing and nothing else crosses, so S25 gives one $D^\sigma$ per energy. With the proven polygon on the adjacent strip and the identity theorem, $D^\sigma$ is the continuation on every connected region where a valid path exists. Outside that region, deeper below the highest flipped threshold, no path with slopes at most 1 is valid; steeper legs then need sampling, without L7.
