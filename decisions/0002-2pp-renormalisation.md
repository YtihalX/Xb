# 2++ is renormalised by the 1++ contact term alone

Decided by: you
Date: 2026-10-28
Source: the grilling session of 2026-10-28

At LO the $2^{++}$ amplitude is renormalised by the $1^{++}$ contact term $C$ alone, acting in $B^*\bar B^*(^5S_2)$; unlike [@Baru:2019xnh] v2, Sec. IV B, no $S$–$D$ counterterm is promoted to LO, because no $2^{++}$ data exist to fix it, even though the lower $B\bar B(^1D_2)$ and $B\bar B^*(^3D_2)$ channels remove the argument [@Zhang:2026iyr] v1, Sec. III B, used in $1^{++}$. The spread of the $X_{b2}$ pole over $\Lambda=3$–$5$ GeV, each with its $1^{++}$ refit, is quoted as part of the LO uncertainty; if the pole changes nature under that variation (crosses a threshold, changes sheet, or merges with the $\chi_{b2}(3P)$-dominated state), the LO prediction is declared unrenormalised and the counterterm question reopens. Spurious poles are projected out when they lie above the $\chi_{b2}(1P)$-dominated state, and otherwise kept with the shift their projection causes reported.

## Considered options

- A numeric stability bound on the pole shift, registered before the cutoff scan.
- Promoting the $S$–$D$ counterterm now, which turns the prediction into a band over an unfixed parameter.

## Consequences

GLOSSARY.md "Spurious pole", "Sharp cutoff"; falsifier: the $X_{b2}$ pole changes nature under the cutoff variation.
