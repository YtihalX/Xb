## Decisions

- **LO contact term** (2026-10-28; user). The $1^{++}$ contact $C$ alone acts in $^5S_2$: no $2^{++}$ data fix an additional $S$–$D$ term. The cutoff spread is quoted as LO uncertainty; if the pole crosses a threshold, changes sheet or merges with the $\chi_{b2}(3P)$-dominated state, the prediction counts as unrenormalised and this choice reopens. A spurious pole above the lowest compact-dominated state is projected out; otherwise it is kept and its projection shift reported.
- **§I scope** (2026-10-28; user). $X_{b2}$ and the dressed $\chi_{b2}(1P,2P,3P)$ spectrum in both fits. Charm and the CMS line shapes come later; the line shapes need that spectrum. Outside this question: $0^{++}$, isovectors, production, radiative decays, isospin breaking, bare-state $D$-wave couplings.
- **Quark model fixed** (2026-10-28; colleague, adopted by the user). The quark model $(m_b,\sigma,\alpha_S,V_0)$ is fixed once for all quantum numbers, with the previously fitted parameters ($\gamma=-\tfrac43\alpha_s$); nothing is refitted in $2^{++}$. The colleague suggested computing the spin-dependent spectrum from the paper's formulas; whether and how far to do so is open (Bare $\chi_{b2}$ masses).
- **$^5D_1$ phase** (2026-10-29; user). The package follows the printed phase of [@Zhang:2026iyr] v1, Eq. (D1); direct angular integration and the printed projected formulas agree with it.
- **Spurious-pole identification** (2026-10-29; user). The spurious pole is the predominantly hadronic member through the avoided crossing.
- **Projection precision** (2026-10-29; user). Projection-strength stability is judged at the paper's 1 MeV precision; the baseline $2P$ spread is $0.104$ MeV.
- **Continuation route** (2026-10-30; user, grilling of 2026-10-29/30; on-shell-point domain sharpened 2026-11-04). Off-physical-sheet poles are reached on a deformed finite loop contour. The on-shell-point route serves as a second route only while no singularity of $V(k_F,q)$ lies between the paths: $|\operatorname{Im}k_F|<m_\pi$ for the exchanged-meson branch points, and about $130$ MeV for the three-body branch point of $PV\leftarrow PV$ near $\operatorname{Re}E=-22.6$ MeV (derived, not checked; `notes/06-xb2-pole-problem.tex`, Sec. 5.3). The sharp cutoff excludes scaling to infinity; keeping the real axis beyond that domain needs explicit cut terms. Branches are tracked with split logs and an independent continuity tracker; complex form factors are bilinear. On-shell bound-state couplings are out of scope, because the Table V poles lie on the OME left-hand cut. The three-body locator serves the admissibility validator now and later charm work, without charm dynamics. An obstruction to a target contour goes back to the user.
- **Singularity map** (2026-10-30; user). The map covered thresholds, left- and right-hand cuts in each channel's complex momentum plane, and the singularities of the LSE integrand, and assumed no pole positions: $X_{b2}$ is not yet determined. It lies in `history/` since 2026-11-04; note 07 carries what the continuation uses, and computes its singular points at probe energies, not at assumed poles.
- **Admissibility** (2026-11-04; user; wording confirmed 2026-11-05). A path is admissible when a homotopy from $[0,\Lambda]$ at an energy with $\operatorname{Im}E_0>0$ reaches it with no propagator pole on the moving path and the kernel analytic with both momenta on it; the kernel takes the branch reached along that homotopy (`CONVENTIONS.md`, "Continuation to other sheets", "Continued angular moments"). A momentum held fixed off the path (on-shell $T$, couplings) needs its own homotopy check, and relations that compare $\mathcal C$ with $[0,\Lambda]$ at fixed momentum need nothing between the paths. Why: every Fredholm term of $D_{\mathcal C}$ is independent of the deformation exactly when the kernel stays analytic on $\mathcal C_\lambda\times\mathcal C_\lambda$. Stage 6 tests this by comparing $D_{\mathcal C}$ on a shallow and a deep path at a narrow probe energy. On 2026-11-05 the user raised that the kernel's singular points move during the homotopy, so that analyticity at every pair seemed impossible; (K) constrains only pairs on the same moving path, which never meet the moving singular points on gentle paths (`notes/07-continued-lse.tex`, commentary after S23, and S35). The user kept the wording; the explanation went into note 07.
- **Moment precision** (2026-11-05; user). The angular moments are computed to $10^{-10}$ relative per moment (`notes/07-continued-lse.tex`, S22, now a definition): double precision above S22's switch, extended precision below it, and extended precision above it too when the measured cancellation gives $16\epsilon\,\kappa>10^{-10}$. Why: the provisional $10^{-12}$ sent almost every record to BigFloat at $2.4\times$ the cost, while the downstream targets ($10^{-6}$ for on-shell $T$, $0.1$ MeV for poles) and the node-discretisation error are far coarser. The double-precision closed form loses digits to cancellation between its pole term and its polynomial, by about $|x_1|^m$ with $x_1=(A-\nu^2)/(2p'q)$, so pairs with one small momentum need extended precision.
